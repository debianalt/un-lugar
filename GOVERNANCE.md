# Governance

This file says who decides what appears on the map. It is a first draft written by one person. It is meant to be rewritten with the people it concerns, and the section on what the people of the place know has no force until they have agreed to it.

## What is on the map today

Open satellite and model products, the author's own tests on them, and dated newspaper reports. All of it comes from public sources. No annotation comes from a community.

## The four registers

Every annotation declares one, and the map never mixes them.

| Register | What it is | Who can add one |
|---|---|---|
| Measured | A reading from an instrument: a gauge, a satellite pass. | Anyone who can name the instrument and the file. |
| Interpreted | Somebody's reading of the data: a model output, a comparison. | Anyone who shows the method. |
| Situated | What people in the place saw or said. | The people concerned, or someone they asked to do it. A public press report counts, marked as such. |
| Speculated | A guess nobody has tested. | Anyone, as long as it says what would test it. |

A statement can change register when evidence arrives. The change is recorded, and the earlier version stays in the history.

## Review

Each annotation shows who checked it and against what. "Nobody yet" is shown as plainly as a review. A reviewer cannot be the only source of the statement they review: the author's own tests are marked "nobody independent yet" until someone else has checked them. Today no note has a recorded independent reviewer: local consultants, university researchers and residents of El Soberbio have read the press reports behind the situated notes, but which note each of them read was not written down, so the page does not name a reviewer for any of them. Disagreement between a note and a layer is kept on the map, since it is what the map is for.

## What the people of the place know

These rules are a proposal to put before the people concerned. Nothing is collected until they have answered.

- **Whoever knows it decides.** A person who contributes a statement decides whether it is shown and how precisely, and can take it back.
- **A neighbourhood speaks through its own body.** A statement about a whole neighbourhood goes through its neighbourhood commission.
- **Indigenous community knowledge is closed by default and kept outside this repository.** It goes through the community's council. This repository is public and so is its history, so that knowledge is not stored here: it stays in tools the community controls, such as CoMapeo or Terrastories, and the map shows only what the community decides to publish.
- **Precision is theirs to set.** A place can be shown exactly, blurred to a cell or a stretch of river, named without a location, or not shown.
- **Withdrawal.** Anything published can be taken back at any time, and it leaves the map at the next build. Once something has been public, copies may exist that nobody can recall. That is said before anything is published, not after.
- **No inference.** The project does not publish where families live, fish or farm from other data when they have not said so themselves.
- **Payment.** People who do the work of recording and reviewing are paid as co-researchers from the project budget.
- **Open licences apply to the code and to public-source data.** They do not apply to what people contribute, which keeps the terms its owners set.

## What is deliberately left out of this repository

Locations of Indigenous communities from any register; attributes of individual buildings, such as estimated residents or informal-settlement status; names of residents quoted in the press.

## Changing these rules

By pull request for the first three sections. The section on what the people of the place know changes only with the agreement of those taking part.
